Ground Truth Dataset and Data-Generating Process
------------------------------------------------
The dataset is a controlled synthetic NSCLC cohort with 1,000 patient-level
records. The clinical question is a comparison of vinorelbine to gemcitabine
for advanced/metastatic NSCLC. Each row contains long generated clinical text
and structured ground-truth columns, including treatment probability, outcome
probability, potential-outcome probabilities, true ITE, true age, and true
PD-L1 expression. The synthetic notes are long: the metadata reports a mean
clinical-text length of 51,718 characters, a mean of 19.2 notes per patient,
and structured data such as encounters, hospitalizations, labs, and PROs.

The causal structure is intentionally simple. Age is the sole true confounder:
it affects both treatment assignment and baseline outcome risk. PD-L1
expression is the sole true effect modifier: it changes the treatment effect
through treatment-by-PD-L1 interaction terms in the outcome equation. The
ground-truth metadata reports a treatment-equation age coefficient of -2.016
and an outcome-equation age coefficient of -1.728 on the generated logit scale.
The outcome equation has a global treatment coefficient of 0.200, plus
treatment interactions of -0.578 for PD-L1 1-49% and 2.077 for PD-L1 >=50%.
Treatment and outcome are still stochastic binary draws from known probabilities,
so even oracle feature recovery does not make the observed labels deterministic.

Workflow
--------
The non-neural agentic forest path uses sparse bag-of-words/TF-IDF models as a
feature-discovery layer rather than as the final causal model. Within each
outer training fold, it fits honest cross-fitted BoW nuisance models for
treatment and outcome prediction. It then uses those nuisance predictions to
compute an unweighted pseudo-effect target and fits another BoW model to find
text features associated with treatment-effect heterogeneity. The top lexical
features from treatment, outcome, and pseudo-effect models are shown to an
agent, which proposes candidate confounders and effect modifiers.

The agentic layer performs candidate proposal, cross-fold consistency review,
alias resolution, feature extraction, and value harmonization. The consistency
step is designed to gate out noisy one-off candidates while allowing recovery
of variables that emerge in weaker inner folds under different names. Extraction
then pulls values for both candidate confounders and candidate effect modifiers
in one pass. After extraction and value harmonization, the final CATE estimator
is an explicit-feature causal forest trained only on extracted structured
features selected inside the relevant outer training fold.

Fold Structure
--------------
Outer folds are used for honest final evaluation: each held-out outer fold is
predicted by a workflow that discovered variables, extracted values, trained
the final causal forest, and produced ITE estimates without using that held-out
fold for model fitting. Inner folds are used within the outer training data for
honest nuisance prediction, pseudo-effect feature discovery, and candidate
consistency checks. In this run, there were 5 outer folds, 5 nuisance folds,
5 effect folds, and 3 inner folds for candidate consistency.

Interpretation
--------------
This run recovered the two important oracle concepts better than the earlier
neural CATE attempts. Age was selected in all 5 folds and always used as a
confounder. PD-L1 was selected in all 5 folds as an effect modifier, although
one fold used the name tumor_pd_l1_expression and four folds used
pd_l1_expression_level. That naming difference should not directly affect the
reported metrics because each outer fold trains and predicts with its own
fold-specific extracted variables, but it does show that alias harmonization
could still be made stricter across folds.

The main remaining limitation is CATE shrinkage/bias. The model captured the
directional high-PD-L1 treatment-benefit signal: the predicted mean ITE for
PD-L1 >=50% was 0.210, clearly above the lower PD-L1 strata. But it compressed
the group means toward a positive average effect. It overpredicted benefit for
PD-L1 1-49% and <1%, and underpredicted benefit for PD-L1 >=50%. This pattern
is consistent with the final causal forest learning real heterogeneity from the
extracted variables, but regularizing or averaging strongly enough that it does
not fully reproduce the oracle subgroup contrasts. The stochastic binary outcome
and treatment assignment also make the final supervised signal noisy even when
the discovered variables are mostly right.
