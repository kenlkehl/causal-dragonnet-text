STAGE 2, FROM START TO FINISH
=============================

Current implementation as of 22 August 2026


WHAT STAGE 2 IS FOR
-------------------

Stage 2 turns clues learned in Stage 1 into an actual causal analysis.

Stage 1 has already read the clinical text through several different modeling
approaches. It hands Stage 2 many small pieces of evidence about patterns that
may matter for treatment choice, outcome, or treatment-effect differences.
Those pieces are not yet a usable patient table. They may say things such as
"older patients," "high biomarker expression," or "brain metastases," but they
do not yet define a clean variable or give every patient a value.

Stage 2 does the following, separately inside each outer fold:

1. Cleans and compresses the Stage 1 evidence without losing its audit trail.
2. Organizes that evidence into a searchable hierarchy of communities.
3. Reads all of the compiled evidence and proposes a broad list of candidate
   patient variables.
4. Uses hierarchical ColBERT retrieval to find the strongest evidence for each
   already-named candidate and reduces the candidate list to a manageable size.
5. Merges aliases, assigns causal roles, and writes a precise extraction rule
   for each surviving variable.
6. Reads the raw clinical record for one patient at a time and extracts those
   frozen variables.
7. Uses only the outer training patients to repair measurement rules, reconcile
   mixed representations, and test whether variables are useful and stable.
8. Freezes the final variables and all value mappings.
9. Extracts the outer-held-out patients once under those frozen rules.
10. Fits causal models and produces held-out treatment-effect predictions.
11. Combines the held-out predictions from all outer folds into the final
    average treatment-effect estimate.

The central separation is this:

    Stage 1 evidence packets -> discover and define variables
    Raw patient documents    -> extract values for those defined variables

Feature discovery does not scan raw patient notes. Raw notes enter only after
the candidate variables and their initial measurement definitions have been
created.


A FEW TERMS USED THROUGHOUT THIS REPORT
---------------------------------------

Outer fold
    One full train/test split. Everything that can adapt -- feature discovery,
    measurement repair, value mapping, and feature selection -- is learned on
    the outer training portion. The outer-held-out patients are reserved for
    the final prediction and effect estimate for that fold.

Inner fold
    A smaller train/validation split made within an outer training fold. Inner
    folds are used to judge whether an extracted variable helps on patients
    that were not used to fit the screening model.

Stage 1 architecture
    One of the distinct Stage 1 evidence-generating approaches. Keeping the
    architectures separate long enough to measure agreement is useful because
    a clue found by several different approaches is more convincing than a clue
    repeated many times by one approach.

Evidence packet
    A JSON record containing scientific evidence from Stage 1, readable text,
    its fold, its source architecture, and provenance. It is evidence about a
    possible variable, not a patient and not an extracted patient value.

Compiled evidence card
    Stage 2's cleaned, deduplicated, and semantically clustered form of related
    Stage 1 evidence. In the code, these cards are also passed around as
    packets. Each compiled card belongs to one outer fold and one Stage 1
    architecture.

Evidence community
    A group of related evidence atoms found by a similarity graph. Communities
    make a compact searchable index. They do not decide which clinical features
    exist.

Serialized community packet
    A community written as one ordinary JSON object, usually one line in a
    JSONL file. "Serialized" merely means converted to a file representation.
    It is not a model-generated patient summary. It contains readable excerpts,
    a full ColBERT routing document, community statistics, and links back to the
    compiled evidence that formed it.

Candidate
    A named possible patient variable, such as age at treatment or biomarker
    expression. A candidate is provisional. It has not necessarily survived
    ranking, causal-role filtering, extraction, or empirical review.

Feature definition, or ontology
    The instructions that turn a candidate into an extractable measurement:
    its name, description, value type, unit or categories, measurement rule,
    and missing-value rule. "Ontology" sounds grander than it is here; it means
    the allowed form and precise meaning of one variable.

Confounder
    A pretreatment variable supported as relevant to both treatment choice and
    outcome.

Prognostic variable
    A pretreatment variable supported as relevant to outcome, but not also
    supported as a treatment-choice variable.

Effect modifier
    A pretreatment variable for which the treatment effect may differ across
    values. One feature can have more than one causal role.

ColBERT
    A text-matching model that compares a short query with a document at the
    token level. It is useful when one small phrase inside a longer evidence
    record matters. Here it is a retrieval and ranking tool, not a clinical
    reasoner and not the source of candidate names.

Router
    A compact community packet used as the first stop in search. Given an
    already-discovered candidate name, a router points Stage 2 toward a smaller
    set of original compiled evidence packets. Those original packets are then
    scored directly.


THE INPUTS STAGE 2 EXPECTS
--------------------------

A full Stage 2 causal run receives four kinds of input:

1. The Stage 1 handoff, containing fold-scoped scientific evidence and its
   provenance.
2. A patient dataset with a stable patient identifier, raw clinical text, a
   binary treatment column, and a numeric outcome column.
3. The outer and inner split provenance, when it is available. If inner splits
   are absent, Stage 2 can make deterministic inner folds within the outer
   training rows.
4. Run configuration: model endpoints, prompt and extraction limits, feature
   limits, random seed, clinical question, and any investigator-specified
   features.

Before analysis begins, Stage 2 checks the modeling columns. Treatment must be
complete and coded 0/1. Outcome must be complete and numeric, and a binary
outcome must also contain only 0/1. It writes a public, redacted copy of the
configuration to config.json.

The standard run expects all supported Stage 1 architectures to be present for
every outer fold. Evidence compilation fails before its first language-model
request if a required architecture is missing. This prevents a partially
produced Stage 1 handoff from silently becoming a different analysis.

Stage 2 can also stop after feature definitions if no patient dataset is
supplied. In that mode it performs evidence compilation, discovery, selection,
consolidation, causal-role assignment, and operationalization, but not patient
extraction or causal estimation.


THE FOLD BOUNDARY: THE MOST IMPORTANT DESIGN RULE
-------------------------------------------------

Each outer fold is its own small analysis. Its feature set is allowed to differ
from the feature sets in other outer folds because it is learned only from that
fold's permitted training evidence and training patients.

For a given outer fold, the following are training-only decisions:

* which candidate variables are discovered;
* which evidence supports each candidate;
* which aliases are merged;
* which causal roles are supported;
* how each variable is measured;
* how mixed numeric and categorical values are harmonized;
* whether extraction failures justify revising a definition;
* whether a variable survives empirical screening; and
* how encoders and causal models are fitted.

The outer-held-out clinical notes are not used to make any of those decisions.
They are extracted only after the definitions and mappings are frozen. Their
outcomes do not go into feature review. This is why the final row-level
predictions can be treated as out-of-fold predictions rather than in-sample
descriptions.

The outer folds may run at the same time, so a directory can look mixed while a
run is in progress: one fold may be discovering candidates while another is
extracting patients. Concurrency does not mix their data. A global request
limit still caps the total number of language-model calls in flight.


STEP 1: COMPILE THE RAW STAGE 1 EVIDENCE
---------------------------------------

The Stage 1 handoff can contain a large number of repeated or closely related
pieces of evidence. Sending every raw occurrence to later steps would waste
context and let frequent duplicates dominate. The evidence compiler therefore
works inside each outer fold and performs three jobs.

First, it extracts only allowlisted scientific content from the supported Stage
1 outputs. Operational details such as file paths, run timing, model mechanics,
or internal identifiers are not treated as clinical evidence.

Second, it removes exact duplication. Exact copies are represented once, but
the member and lineage files retain where every occurrence came from. Repeated
text can therefore increase provenance support without consuming the prompt
again and again.

Third, it conservatively groups semantically similar evidence into compiled
cards. The compiler stratifies this work so that one prolific architecture,
evidence kind, causal axis, or polarity does not take the entire allowance.
Each compiled card remains tied to exactly one Stage 1 architecture. The normal
cap is 400 cards per outer fold, although a run configuration can change it.

A card contains a small number of readable representative excerpts plus
machine-readable support and provenance. The compiler records both the compact
card and its membership, so a later reviewer can trace the card back through
the exact Stage 1 occurrences.

The main compilation artifacts are under evidence_compilation/. For each outer
fold, cards.jsonl contains the compiled cards, members.jsonl contains their
members, and lineage.jsonl records the chain back to Stage 1. Summary and
completion files record counts and input fingerprints.

This is a compression step, not feature discovery. No clinical variable has
yet been named.


STEP 2: BUILD AN INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE-COMMUNITY HIERARCHY
--------------------------------------------------------

Stage 2 next builds a searchable map of the compiled evidence. This map is used
later to find evidence for an already-named candidate. Under the current
candidate-first design, it is not the input gate for candidate discovery.
Discovery reads all compiled cards by default.

The hierarchy is built without a feature query. There is no query for age,
PD-L1, or any other clinical concept at this point.

2.1 Split readable evidence into short atoms

The readable representatives are split into overlapping spans of at most 16
words, with a 4-word overlap by default. Short atoms reduce the chance that one
small but important phrase is drowned out by the rest of a long excerpt.

Each atom keeps its source packet, architecture, evidence axes, inner-fold
support, and exact-member provenance. The text is shortened for matching, but
the links needed to reconstruct support are retained.

2.2 Find likely neighbors cheaply

The atom encoder first produces pooled vectors. Those vectors are used only to
make a manageable neighbor shortlist. In the first round, an atom looks for
neighbors from other Stage 1 architectures. This favors agreement across
independent evidence-generating approaches rather than clusters made entirely
of one architecture repeating itself.

2.3 Rerank with symmetric document-to-document ColBERT

ColBERT then compares the shortlisted atom pairs at the token level. Because
both sides are evidence documents rather than a query and a document, the code
uses a symmetric MeanMaxSim comparison. An edge is kept only when the atoms are
among each other's top neighbors. The default reciprocal neighborhood is five.

This reciprocal rule makes the graph conservative: a one-way resemblance is
not enough.

2.4 Form first-level communities

The reciprocal graph is clustered with Louvain community detection. The result
is a set of small, related evidence groups. The number of first-level
communities depends on the evidence; it is not a fixed constant.

2.5 Coarsen the communities in later rounds

Later hierarchy rounds render every underlying atom in a community into one
complete community document, re-encode those documents, compare communities,
and cluster again. These later rounds do not require cross-architecture pairs,
because each lower-level community already carries its full source mixture.

The default target counts are approximately 300 communities and then 75. They
are targets for choosing a clustering resolution, not promises of an exact
count. A round is skipped if its target is not smaller than its input. The
artifacts record the actual count at every level.

2.6 Choose the final router packets

The final serialized set is capped at 75 community packets per outer fold by
default. Selection reserves room for evidence useful to the confounder lane and
the effect-modifier lane -- normally 30 for each -- then deduplicates overlaps
and fills remaining slots by the overall community score. These lanes are
derived from generic Stage 1 evidence axes, not from named clinical concepts.

Community scores reward such things as support, architecture diversity,
inner-fold coverage, causal-axis relevance, and graph cohesion. They do not
contain hand-written bonuses for age, PD-L1, or another target variable.

2.7 Serialize the result

Each selected community becomes one JSON packet containing:

* readable representative evidence;
* consensus phrases;
* counts and coverage summaries;
* the community's causal lanes and evidence axes;
* source packet identifiers; and
* a ColBERT document containing the community's complete atom text for later
  routing.

These packets are written under evidence_communities/, along with the atoms,
graph edges, first-level communities, hierarchy records, selection details,
and summary. packets.jsonl is the compact set used as candidate routers.

Again, the current default is important: these community packets help route
named candidates later. They do not replace the compiled cards as the source
read by the discovery prompts.


STEP 3: HIGH-RECALL, CANDIDATE-FIRST FEATURE DISCOVERY
-----------------------------------------------------

Candidate discovery now starts from every compiled evidence card in the outer
fold. This is why it is called candidate-first: Stage 2 asks what variables are
present before using a feature name as a search query.

3.1 Keep architectures visible to the scheduler

Compiled cards are grouped by their Stage 1 architecture and divided into
batches that fit the exact rendered prompt-character limit. Architecture is
used by Python to schedule and audit the batches. The model itself is not told
which architecture produced a batch.

3.2 Show the model only readable evidence

For each card, the discovery prompt contains a temporary item number and the
readable consensus text and representative excerpts. It does not expose packet
IDs, architecture names, support counts, evidence scores, fold labels,
polarities, causal-axis metadata, or the clinical question. Those fields could
encourage the model to follow analysis metadata rather than the clinical text.

The prompt asks for every atomic patient-level clinical variable supported by
each item. It explicitly asks the model to notice demographic clauses,
biomarkers, laboratory measurements, disease sites, clinical history, and
other secondary details instead of returning only the dominant topic. It also
rejects names, administrative fields, documentation artifacts, modeling
methods, cohort-level summaries, and vague composite inventories.

The instruction is generic. It does not name PD-L1, age, or another desired
answer. The configuration's explicit_features list is empty unless an
investigator deliberately supplies a feature.

At this stage the model supplies only:

* a snake_case candidate name;
* a short description; and
* the temporary item numbers that support it.

It does not choose a data type, unit, list of categories, or patient extraction
rule yet.

3.3 Map citations back to evidence

Python validates every citation and maps each prompt-local item number back to
the real compiled packet. A candidate with no valid cited packet is not
accepted. The code then attaches the packet's architectures and evidence axes
outside the prompt. This is how readable discovery remains connected to exact
provenance.

3.4 Audit evidence that produced no candidate

After the first pass, every compiled card that was not cited by any candidate
is sent through a separate recall-oriented audit. The audit again asks for all
atomic patient variables, including secondary details that may not match the
card's apparent topic. Candidates from the initial pass and audit are merged.

This second pass matters because an evidence card can contain several facts.
The most salient fact may be found on the first pass while a short demographic
or biomarker clause is overlooked.

The resulting raw list is saved in interpreted_candidates.json. This file is
the first place to inspect when asking whether a concept was ever discovered.


STEP 4: BUILD A CONSERVATIVE CANDIDATE REGISTRY
-----------------------------------------------

The same measurement can appear under several spellings. Before ranking,
Stage 2 creates a registry of canonical candidate names.

It first collapses exact normalized names. It can then use dense embeddings to
merge extremely similar names, with a default similarity threshold of 0.94.
Dense similarity alone is not sufficient: the names must also share an
informative lexical anchor. Generic words that occur everywhere are not
accepted as anchors.

This is deliberately conservative. "Age," "age at diagnosis," and "patient
age" may be recognized as one family when the names support that reading, but
two clinically different measurements should not be merged merely because
their embeddings are nearby.

Every registry entry retains all original candidate IDs, descriptions,
citations, source architectures, and evidence axes. Registry construction
reduces aliases; it does not discard provenance.


STEP 5: USE HIERARCHICAL COLBERT TO SUPPORT AND RANK NAMED CANDIDATES
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Only now does a feature name become a ColBERT query. For example, a discovered
identifier such as patient_age is rendered as the short query "Patient Age."
The query contains the candidate name and nothing more elaborate. This point is
why hierarchical retrieval cannot cause an undiscovered concept to appear: the
concept must already have been named from compiled evidence before a query
exists.

5.1 Search the community routers

The candidate name is scored against the final community packets. By default,
the three best communities are selected. This configuration value --
candidate_selection_hierarchy_top_communities -- controls breadth. Raising it
means that each candidate descends through more communities and therefore
examines more original compiled packets; it also costs more matching time and
admits more weakly related material.

5.2 Descend to original compiled evidence

Each selected community lists the compiled packets from which it came. Stage 2
forms a leaf-level search set from those source packets. It always adds the
packets that the discovery model directly cited, even if no selected community
would have routed to them. Direct discovery evidence therefore cannot be lost
because the hierarchy placed it oddly.

5.3 Rerank the leaves

ColBERT scores the candidate name directly against every compiled packet in the
routed leaf set. The default top three leaf packets become the candidate's main
ontology evidence. A small relevant phrase can score well even when it sits in
a longer card, which is the main reason for using late interaction here.

The router is only an efficient first pass. The final evidence score comes from
the original compiled cards, not from assuming that every item in a broad
community is equally relevant.

5.4 Add independent coverage information

Stage 2 calculates architecture coverage and inner-fold coverage from packets
that discovery directly cited. It does not inflate coverage by counting every
descendant of a broad router community.

The default aggregate support score is:

    75%  normalized mean relevance of the top evidence packets
    12.5% fraction of Stage 1 architectures directly supporting the candidate
    12.5% fraction of inner folds directly supporting the candidate

5.5 Preserve several causal evidence lanes, then apply the hard cap

Candidates are ranked within the evidence axes carried from Stage 1: treatment,
outcome, residual effect, matched pair, semantic, and unclear. The normal
configuration keeps up to the top 50 per axis, takes an axis-balanced union, and
then applies a hard limit of 50 discovered candidates per outer fold.

This is the main narrowing point before alias consolidation. A real concept can
be discovered but still fall below the fold cap. The selection file records
both retained and dropped registry candidates, their scores, their routed
communities, their top evidence, and all original candidate IDs affected.

The main artifacts are:

* candidate_registry_selection.json -- registry merges, scores, routes,
  rankings, retained candidates, and dropped candidates;
* selected_candidates.json -- the candidates that passed the cap;
* input_packets.jsonl -- the full compiled evidence used for discovery; and
* colbert_support_packets.jsonl -- the bounded hierarchy routers used after
  candidate naming.


HOW TO TRACE A MISSED FEATURE
-----------------------------

The design makes a miss traceable as a sequence rather than a guess. Starting
from the beginning, ask:

1. Was the relevant Stage 1 occurrence present in the handoff?
2. Did evidence compilation put it into a card, and does members.jsonl or
   lineage.jsonl preserve the occurrence?
3. Is the readable phrase present in input_packets.jsonl for that outer fold?
4. Did interpreted_candidates.json contain a candidate for it, either on the
   first pass or the rejected-card audit?
5. If it was discovered, was it merged into a registry entry under another
   name?
6. Did candidate_registry_selection.json retain it, or did the 50-candidate
   ranking cap remove it?
7. Did alias consolidation merge it correctly, and did the merged evidence
   preserve all of its cited packets?
8. Did the deterministic causal-role filter find a supported role?
9. Did operationalization define the intended measurement, or fall back to an
   ambiguous definition after validation failure?
10. During extraction, was the concept absent, represented unexpectedly, or
    rejected by a closed-category rule?
11. During empirical review, was it dropped for poor measurement or lack of
    stable training-fold signal?

That path distinguishes four very different statements: "Stage 1 never saw
it," "discovery failed to name it," "ranking removed it," and "it was measured
but did not survive review."


STEP 6: CONSOLIDATE TRUE ALIASES WITHOUT USING THE MODEL AS A FILTER
-------------------------------------------------------------------

The selected candidates now go through a broader alias-consolidation process.
This is separate from the conservative registry merge because aliases may be
worded differently enough to require clinical interpretation.

The consolidation model sees bounded batches, normally 20 candidate groups at
a time. The first rounds use shifted alphabetical order. Later rounds use
deterministic seeded shuffles, for up to 55 rounds by default. Varying the
neighborhood lets aliases meet even if they were far apart in one ordering.

The model's authority in this step is narrow: it may merge candidates that mean
the same scalar measurement. It may not exclude a candidate, invent a new
feature, split one candidate into several, or discard cited evidence. Python
checks that every input candidate has exactly one disposition and that a merge
preserves all packet support.

A deterministic lexical measurement-anchor check blocks semantically
incompatible merges. If a model response is invalid, the batch falls back to
passing the groups through rather than silently losing them.

Investigator-specified explicit features, if any, are added after the discovered
candidate cap. They cannot be renamed, merged into a different measurement,
revised, or dropped by the ordinary pipeline.


STEP 7: ASSIGN CAUSAL ROLES FROM STAGE 1 SUPPORT
-----------------------------------------------

After aliases have been consolidated, Python assigns roles from the evidence
axes attached to all supporting packets:

* Treatment plus outcome support gives the feature the confounder role.
* Outcome support without treatment support gives it the prognostic role.
* Residual-effect or matched-pair support gives it the effect-modifier role.

A feature may be both a confounder and an effect modifier, for example. A group
with none of the supported roles is excluded and recorded in
consolidation/causal_role_filter.json.

This step is deterministic. A language model does not look at the clinical
question and freely declare roles. The roles must be backed by the fold-local
Stage 1 evidence axes.


STEP 8: TURN EACH SURVIVING NAME INTO AN EXTRACTION DEFINITION
-------------------------------------------------------------

Each retained group is operationalized one feature at a time. The model sees
the canonical feature name and readable text from the selected evidence
packets. It does not see packet IDs, architecture labels, fold numbers, support
scores, causal axes, roles, or the clinical question.

It must return one flat definition containing:

* a plain description of the one patient-level measurement;
* value type: binary, categorical, continuous, ordinal, or ambiguous;
* allowed categories or the unit;
* the measurement definition;
* the missing-value rule;
* a short scientific support summary; and
* caveats.

The rule prefers continuous measurement when a realistic number exists, but it
does not force false precision. A continuous definition may explicitly allow a
documented category or threshold string when an exact number is missing. This
is important for biomarkers that may be reported as an exact percentage in one
record and as "less than 1%," "1-49%," or "50% or greater" in another.

Binary and closed categorical definitions are validated strictly. A binary
feature must have exactly two distinct scalar ontology values. Categorical and
ordinal features must have at least two distinct values, with no duplicate
spellings disguised by punctuation or capitalization.

Malformed responses are repaired within bounded retry limits. If a valid,
grounded definition still cannot be produced, Stage 2 uses a conservative
ambiguous definition rather than crashing the entire fold or pretending to
know the answer.

At this point feature_definitions.json and definitions_complete.json mark the
end of definition discovery. If the run has no patient dataset, Stage 2 stops
here.


STEP 9: EXTRACT THE DEFINITIONS FROM RAW PATIENT DOCUMENTS
---------------------------------------------------------

This is the first stage that reads the raw patient clinical text.

Extraction is intentionally narrow. Every request contains exactly one
patient's record. Definitions are divided into stable slices, normally no more
than 10 features per request. No prompt ever contains several patients, so the
model cannot compare patients or copy a value from one row into another.

For each feature, the extractor receives the frozen name, type, allowed values,
measurement definition, and missing-value rule. It must return one scalar or
null. It is told to use only the supplied clinical record and to return null
when the record does not support a value.

For binary, categorical, and ordinal features, the returned scalar must match
one declared ontology value exactly. Validation and bounded repair occur at the
feature level, so one invalid field can be set to null without throwing away
valid fields from the same patient.

If a patient note is too large for one prompt, Stage 2 splits it into contiguous,
lossless pages and prefers nearby note, paragraph, line, sentence, or word
boundaries. Page extraction returns every supported observation with an exact
evidence quote, verified page-relative and document-relative character offsets,
and a locally normalized date backed by an exact source quote when one is
explicit. It does not collapse conflicting longitudinal values within a page.

Reconciliation is then local and deterministic rather than another LLM call.
The frozen feature ontology specifies latest, earliest, maximum, minimum, mode,
any-positive, or conservative null-on-conflict behavior. Verified dates take
precedence for temporal rules, source order is the explicit fallback, and the
complete observation set and selected observation ID are written to an audit
file. A missing observation on one page cannot overwrite evidence from another,
and no page is silently dropped.

Work is checkpointed at the patient, feature-slice, page, repair, and
reconciliation levels. An interrupted run can reuse successful calls.


STEP 10: LEARN FROM REPEATED EXTRACTION FAILURES, USING TRAINING PATIENTS ONLY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every extraction writes an issue ledger. It distinguishes a whole-response
format failure from a feature-specific ontology failure such as an unexpected
category.

If the same feature fails for several outer-training patients -- three by
default -- Stage 2 may ask for a one-feature ontology refinement. The refinement
request sees:

* the current definition;
* counts of failure kinds; and
* the failed output forms.

It does not see the patients' clinical text, treatment, outcome, or any
outer-held-out information. It can clarify the description, type, categories,
unit, measurement rule, and missing-value rule. It cannot change the feature's
identity, name, causal roles, or supporting evidence. The default limit is two
refinement rounds.

This feedback loop addresses systematic representation mistakes -- for
example, an ontology that allowed "positive" while the records consistently
say "detected" -- without allowing patient outcomes to steer the wording.
Investigator-specified explicit features are immutable; their failures are
audited but do not rewrite their ontology.


STEP 11: HARMONIZE CONTINUOUS VALUES THAT ARRIVE IN MIXED FORMS
--------------------------------------------------------------

Clinical records often mix exact numbers and categories. Stage 2 deliberately
allows this at extraction time instead of forcing the model to invent a number.

For a continuous feature, a valid raw extraction can therefore be either:

* a numeric scalar; or
* a concise, documented categorical or threshold string permitted by the
  measurement rule.

After outer-training extraction, Stage 2 summarizes numeric values and fallback
strings separately. It then learns a harmonization plan using only those
training observations.

There are three main outcomes.

1. Continuous representation

   If every fallback string has an exact, defensible numeric meaning, it can be
   mapped without inventing a midpoint and the feature remains continuous.

2. Categorical representation

   If the strings describe ranges or categories rather than exact values,
   Stage 2 can convert the training numbers into matching bins and map the
   strings into the same exhaustive category set. For a biomarker percentage,
   a learned plan might use categories such as less than 1%, 1-49%, and at
   least 50%. That is an example of a plan learned from observed training
   representations, not a category list hard-coded into discovery.

3. Safe hybrid fallback

   If a common validated mapping cannot be learned, Stage 2 keeps a numeric
   channel plus indicators for numeric presence or missingness and one-hot
   channels for observed string categories, including an other category. It
   does not coerce a range such as "1-49%" to a made-up midpoint.

The chosen plan is attached to the feature definition and frozen. The same
mapping is later applied deterministically to outer-held-out extractions.
Ambiguous, conflicting, or previously unseen held-out strings become null or
the plan's declared safe fallback; held-out values do not teach the plan new
categories.

Later training review rounds may extend an exact training token map when new
training extractions reveal an equivalent spelling, but they cannot use
held-out records to change the representation.


STEP 12: SUMMARIZE MEASUREMENT QUALITY ON THE OUTER TRAINING FOLD
---------------------------------------------------------------

Stage 2 calculates a compact summary for every extracted feature, including:

* number and fraction nonmissing;
* number of unique values;
* most common value and its fraction;
* common values;
* numeric range and distribution information when appropriate; and
* counts of numeric values versus categorical fallback strings.

These summaries reveal variables that are nearly all missing, invariant, or
dominated by one value. The normal guides are at least 5% nonmissing and no more
than 98% in one dominant value, although the final decision also uses causal
role and predictive evidence.


STEP 13: SCREEN FEATURES WITH INNER-HELD-OUT RANDOM FORESTS
----------------------------------------------------------

Measurement plausibility is not enough. Stage 2 tests whether each claimed role
has empirical support on inner-held-out patients within the outer training
fold.

The default screening family is random forest with 200 trees. For each inner
fold, models are fitted on the inner training rows and scored on the inner
validation rows. Stage 2 compares a no-feature baseline with models using the
extracted features.

Roles determine where a feature is used:

* Confounders enter the treatment-propensity model.
* All retained features can enter the outcome models.
* Effect modifiers enter the treatment-effect model.

Stage 2 records treatment prediction, observed-outcome prediction, and
treatment-effect R-loss. It orients improvement numbers so that positive always
means better, even when the underlying metric is an error or loss.

It evaluates the complete feature set, every feature by itself, and the full
set with one feature removed. The singleton screen asks whether a feature can
support its claimed role on its own. The leave-one-out screen asks what it adds
in the presence of the other retained features.

Role support is defined plainly:

* A confounder needs positive treatment and outcome signal.
* A prognostic variable needs positive outcome signal.
* An effect modifier needs positive treatment-effect R-loss signal.

Aggregate improvement alone is not enough; support must also occur in a
sufficient fraction of the inner folds.


STEP 14: REPEAT THE SCREEN TO MEASURE STABILITY
----------------------------------------------

One random-forest screen can be noisy. Stage 2 accumulates votes for the same
feature representation across evaluation rounds. By default, an ordinary role
needs at least three evaluations and positive support in at least two-thirds of
them before it is stably retained.

Effect modifiers use a deliberately asymmetric rule. Heterogeneous effects are
hard to detect, so absence of a positive signal is not enough to remove a
modifier. It is pruned only when repeated screens show a consistently and
materially worse treatment-effect R-loss beyond the configured negative
margin.

If a measurement rule, value type, harmonization plan, or modeling strategy
changes, its stability history is tied to the old representation and does not
count as proof for the new one.


STEP 15: BOUNDED LANGUAGE-MODEL REVIEW, FOLLOWED BY DETERMINISTIC PRUNING
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The reviewer receives only outer-training information: the clinical question,
the current definitions, extraction summaries, inner-validation performance,
singleton signals, leave-one-out results, and stability state.

It can keep or drop a feature. In an early review it may also clarify how the
same measurement is extracted. It cannot add a new feature, change supporting
evidence, or assign a new causal role. The final allowed review cannot revise a
definition, because a revised definition would otherwise leave the loop
without being evaluated.

There are at most two language-model review rounds by default. Deterministic
evaluation-only rounds may continue after those reviews until the feature set
and stability decisions stop changing, up to ten evaluation rounds in total.

After each review, Python applies the empirical gates. A model request to drop
a variable can be overridden when the variable has protected stable support.
Ordinary roles without stable positive evidence can be removed. Effect
modifiers follow the conservative negative-evidence rule described above.

Any change to a definition, representation, role, or retained set triggers
another training extraction and evaluation as needed. A fold is marked
converged only when definitions are unchanged and stability selection is
complete. If the ten-round limit is reached first, the fold is flagged as
non-converged and proceeds with its latest retained definitions rather than
hiding the condition.


STEP 16: FREEZE THE FEATURE SET
-------------------------------

When review ends, Stage 2 writes final_definitions.json. If the last evaluation
used exactly those definitions, its training extraction can be reused. If the
final review changed the set or representation, Stage 2 re-extracts the outer
training patients so that the final table exactly matches the final rules.

Before continuing, it checks extraction health. The final definitions,
harmonization plans, causal roles, and missing-value behavior are now fixed for
the fold. The later encoders are fitted from this frozen outer-training table;
they do not learn categories or scaling rules from held-out patients.


STEP 17: EXTRACT THE OUTER-HELD-OUT PATIENTS ONCE
------------------------------------------------

Only after the freeze does Stage 2 read the outer-held-out clinical documents.
It uses the same one-patient, bounded-feature extraction procedure. It then
applies the frozen training-fold harmonization plan without learning from the
held-out distribution.

The held-out outcomes are not sent to the extractor, ontology refiner,
harmonizer, or reviewer. A strange held-out spelling is not a reason to reopen
the feature definition. This strict behavior can create nulls, but it preserves
the meaning of held-out evaluation.

The training and held-out extracted tables are joined by the internal row ID
and written as extracted_features.csv for the fold.


STEP 18: FIT THE FOLD'S CAUSAL MODELS
------------------------------------

The final estimator uses random forests and role-aware feature matrices.
Categorical variables are one-hot encoded. Continuous and hybrid variables use
their frozen modeling strategy and missingness handling.

Inside the outer training fold, Stage 2 first makes cross-fitted nuisance
predictions using the inner folds:

* propensity: probability of receiving treatment;
* mu0: predicted outcome under no treatment; and
* mu1: predicted outcome under treatment.

Cross-fitting means each outer-training row receives nuisance predictions from
models that did not train on that row. Those predictions are used to construct
doubly robust pseudo-outcomes for the treatment-effect model.

Stage 2 then fits final propensity and outcome forests on all outer-training
rows and predicts the outer-held-out rows. The propensity model uses
confounders. The outcome models use all retained features. Propensity values are
protected from division by extreme probabilities with the configured clip,
0.02 by default.

An effect forest is fitted on the cross-fitted training pseudo-outcomes using
the effect-modifier features. It predicts a conditional treatment effect, or
CATE, for each held-out patient. If there are no effect modifiers, the model
still produces a constant-style effect prediction rather than inventing
heterogeneity.

For every outer-held-out row, Stage 2 writes:

* treatment and outcome;
* predicted propensity;
* predicted outcome under no treatment, mu0;
* predicted outcome under treatment, mu1;
* an estimated conditional treatment effect; and
* an augmented inverse-probability-weighted, or AIPW, score.

The AIPW score combines outcome modeling and propensity weighting. It is called
doubly robust because the estimate can remain consistent when one of those two
nuisance components is correctly specified, under the usual causal assumptions.
That statistical property does not repair unmeasured confounding, bad temporal
ordering, or positivity violations.


STEP 19: COMBINE OUTER FOLDS INTO THE FINAL ESTIMATE
---------------------------------------------------

Each patient must appear in exactly one outer-held-out prediction file. Stage 2
checks that the union covers every dataset row once and only once, sorts the
rows, and writes cross_fitted_predictions.csv.

The final average treatment effect is the mean of all finite held-out AIPW
scores. Its standard error is the empirical standard deviation of those scores
divided by the square root of the number of patients. The reported 95%
confidence interval uses plus or minus 1.96 standard errors.

causal_estimate.json records the average treatment effect, standard error,
confidence interval, mean estimated CATE, row count, and prediction path.

In synthetic datasets, a true individual treatment effect may be present for
evaluation. Stage 2 joins that oracle column only after all predictions are
frozen and writes separate post-hoc metrics. Oracle values never enter feature
discovery, extraction, selection, model fitting, or the causal estimate.


WHAT THE LANGUAGE MODEL DECIDES, AND WHAT ORDINARY CODE DECIDES
--------------------------------------------------------------

The language model is used where reading clinical language is necessary:

* naming candidate variables from readable Stage 1 evidence;
* auditing evidence that produced no candidate;
* recognizing aliases in bounded batches;
* writing one feature's extraction definition;
* extracting frozen features from one patient's text;
* reconciling pages of one long note;
* repairing repeated ontology failures; and
* reviewing training-fold measurement quality and model diagnostics.

Ordinary deterministic code controls:

* fold boundaries;
* evidence allowlists and provenance;
* exact deduplication and compilation limits;
* graph construction and community selection;
* prompt batching and patient isolation;
* response schemas and citation validation;
* candidate registry rules and semantic-merge safeguards;
* ColBERT routing and score calculation;
* the 50-candidate cap;
* causal-role derivation;
* closed-category validation;
* harmonization-plan application;
* stability votes and pruning gates;
* all joins, encoders, random forests, causal scores, and final aggregation; and
* checkpoint fingerprints and completion rules.

The model therefore reads and labels text, but it does not have unchecked
authority over the analysis.


NO HIDDEN PD-L1 OR AGE HINTS IN THE STANDARD PATH
-------------------------------------------------

The standard candidate discovery prompts contain generic instructions to find
all atomic patient-level clinical variables. They do not mention PD-L1 or age.
The pre-discovery community builder is also query-free: it compares evidence
documents with other evidence documents.

After discovery, the natural-language name of each discovered candidate becomes
a ColBERT query. If the model has already named a candidate PD-L1 expression,
then "PD-L1 Expression" will of course appear as its retrieval query. That is
not leakage into discovery; it is the discovered candidate being used to find
its own strongest evidence.

Similarly, causal lanes use generic evidence labels such as treatment, outcome,
residual effect, and matched pair. They contain no secret clinical-concept list.

An investigator can deliberately provide explicit features in configuration.
Those are visible, protected inputs and should be reported as prespecified, not
as discoveries. With explicit_features empty, the standard production path has
no non-prompt age or PD-L1 seed.


CHECKPOINTING, RESUMING, AND SAFE RERUNS
---------------------------------------

Stage 2 is file-oriented and heavily checkpointed. A successful unit of work is
marked complete only after its validated output has been written. Language-model
requests, pages, patient-feature slices, review rounds, fold estimates, and
top-level products can all be reused.

Every important checkpoint has an input fingerprint. If the evidence,
definition policy, explicit features, or other material configuration differs
from a completed fold, Stage 2 fails closed and asks for a fresh output
directory instead of mixing two analyses.

If the Stage 2 output directory is removed and the same command is run again,
the pipeline can rebuild it from the Stage 1 handoff and source data. If the
directory is retained, the same command resumes compatible work and skips
completed units. Deleting output is not required for an ordinary interrupted
run and gives up useful checkpoints and audit history.

Transport failures use bounded retries. Invalid JSON or schema violations use
bounded repairs. When a safe local fallback exists, it is conservative: keep
groups separate, mark a definition ambiguous, or set one value to null. Stage 2
does not silently invent clinical values to make a fold finish.

WHAT THIS PIPELINE CAN AND CANNOT CLAIM
---------------------------------------

Stage 2 is designed to make feature discovery broad, provenance-preserving,
fold-local, and auditable. Candidate-first discovery prevents a small set of
preselected community topics from deciding what is allowed to exist.
Hierarchical ColBERT makes evidence retrieval practical after names have been
found. The extraction and review loops then force those names to survive contact
with actual patient records and held-out predictive checks.

Those safeguards do not prove that every relevant confounder was documented or
discovered. A feature can still be absent from the source text, missed by Stage
1, compressed poorly, overlooked by both discovery passes, ranked below the
candidate cap, assigned no supported causal role, measured unreliably, or
pruned by training-fold evidence. The artifact chain is intended to show which
of those happened.

Nor does predictive usefulness by itself establish causal validity. Correct
temporal ordering, consistency, exchangeability, positivity, appropriate
outcome definition, and absence of important unmeasured confounding remain
scientific assumptions. Stage 2 helps operationalize and audit a causal
analysis; it does not make those assumptions disappear.


ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION
---------------------

For each outer fold, Stage 2 cleans the Stage 1 evidence into traceable cards
and independently builds a query-free ColBERT community hierarchy. A language
model reads every compiled card, names all grounded patient variables it can
find, and audits initially unused cards. Python conservatively merges duplicate
names. Each discovered name then queries the hierarchy, descends to original
cards, and receives a support score; an axis-balanced ranking keeps at most 50
candidates. A bounded alias process merges true synonyms, deterministic Stage
1 axes assign causal roles, and each retained variable receives a precise
measurement rule. Only then does Stage 2 read raw notes, one patient at a time.
Using outer-training patients only, it repairs systematic ontology problems,
harmonizes mixed values, evaluates variables on inner-held-out rows, and repeats
until selection stabilizes or the round limit is reached. It freezes the final
definitions, extracts the outer-held-out notes once, fits role-aware random
forests, and writes held-out AIPW and CATE predictions. Finally it checks that
every patient was held out exactly once and averages the AIPW scores to obtain
the reported treatment effect.
